# Decisions log

Choices made without asking during the autonomous run of Phases 25–29 (the user asked me
not to stop for questions). Each entry says what was decided and why. Newest at the bottom.

## Phase 25: Character select
- **Showroom layout:** each player's panel shows the character standing on a podium *and* the chosen kart on a turntable with that character driving, side by side, so one preview covers both choices.
- **Poses:** the pirate pack has no dedicated "victory" clip, so picking a character plays **Wave** and pressing Ready plays **Yes** (a cheer). The code-built animals get a code-made idle (breathing and sway) and a happy spinning hop.
- **Rendering:** the previews are drawn by the game's own 3D renderer into each panel's preview box (the canvas is see-through during the select screen), not by extra WebGL canvases, so models load once.
- **Short screens:** a compact layout below 640 px tall, so a 960×540 laptop window fits everything.

## Phase 26: Design pass (impeccable audit → polish, Emil's animation review)
- **No product interview:** impeccable's setup wants to ask the user about the product first (PRODUCT.md). You asked me not to stop for questions, so I ran it as a *refinement* of the existing look (which the tool allows) and did not invent a new visual direction.
- **Audit (before → after, 0–4 each):** accessibility 2 → 3 (secondary text was 3.1:1, now ≥ 4.5:1 on console and phone; blue links darkened), performance 3 → 3 (two layout-property animations moved to transform), theming 2 → 2 (no dark mode is needed for a living-room TV page; colors still partly hard-coded per game), responsive 3 → 3, implementation integrity 3 → 3.
- **Kept on purpose (detector flags):** the red-and-white stripes (track curbs on the phone's steering strip) and the speed lines come from the racing world, so they stay. The "glow" flag on the neutral elevation shadow is a false positive for a light page. The "image without src" flags are the QR codes, now hidden until loaded.
- **Motion:** panels at rare, important moments (ready / game over / results / pause / mode picker) now arrive in 240 ms from 96 % scale; short messages ("Goal!", "GO!", "Trick!") punch in over 180 ms. Frequent things (hovering, D-pad moves) stay instant or near-instant. Reduced motion = a short fade only.
- **Balloon Pop HUD:** score and timer now sit on white pills like the 3D games' scoreboards, for one consistent HUD language.

## Phase 27: New kart items
- **My original item is the Magnet:** for 6 seconds you pick up coins from 10 m away instead of 2 m, and you get a slipstream (up to +4 m/s) toward the kart just ahead when it's within 30 m. Chosen because coins already raise top speed in our game, so the Magnet feeds an existing system instead of adding a new one, and it rewards chasing rather than attacking.
- **Lightning Bolt:** shrinks every kart *ahead* of the user for 3.5 s (top speed ×0.72, much lighter in bumps, speed ×0.6 at the strike), with no spin-out, so it's strong but not cruel. A Shield blocks it and is used up. Karts that already finished aren't touched. Only karts near the back can get it (15 % at the back, never at the front).
- **Triple Slick:** stays in your item box with a "×3" counter; each ITEM press drops one slick. The computer drops its three about 0.7 s apart.
- **Odds** were rebalanced so the new items appear without crowding out the old ones (see `ITEM_ODDS` in karts-data.js).

## Phase 28: Glide-ramp track (Cloud Gardens)
- **Theme:** gardens above the clouds (pink-and-blue sky, puffy clouds, floating rock islands with lawns, trees). Original look, built from code plus the trees we already ship.
- **What a "shortcut" is:** the road stays one closed loop (so lap counting, race places and the barriers keep working). A shortcut ramp is a narrow ramp on *one side* of the road, on the side facing the landing. Taking it is a choice: line up on that side or drive past. The flight goes straight across the gap and lands further along the same lap, skipping a long loop of road (~110 m of flight saves ~120 m of road, about 4 s).
- **Flight rules:** during a shortcut flight the kart's lap position follows how far across the gap it is, so race places update smoothly and the finish line can't be skipped. An invisible arc (6 m high in the middle) keeps the glider from sinking into the gap, and it keeps at least 20 m/s, so a hit in mid-air can't leave anyone stuck. Glowing orange hoops show the line, and the minimap draws dashed lines.
- **Ramps on this track:** three in total: one full-width glide down from the hilltop (blue posts) and two shortcut ramps (orange hoops on their posts).
- **Computer racers** take a shortcut about 45 % of the time (decided once per ramp per lap), so they stay beatable but you'll see them use it.

## Phase 29: Boot animation and two new channels
- **Boot logo:** a rounded TV screen with a little couch inside; its four cushions are the four player colors. It says "everyone on the couch, one screen" without borrowing anything. Timeline: the screen outline draws itself (0.6 s), the cushions settle onto the couch one by one, the screen lights up with a chime, then "Couch Console" rises in and fades out at 2.7 s. All ease-out, no bounce (the old intro's springy curve was flagged in the design pass). Skippable with a click, any key or any phone button; with reduced motion everything just appears and it ends after 1.2 s.
- **Channel 1: Timer** (Clock · Timer · Stopwatch). Why: it works with no internet at all, and a living-room console is exactly where you need a big visible countdown: turn timers for board games, "five more minutes", cooking while the others race. When it runs out, every connected phone buzzes, so nobody misses it. It keeps running while you play something else.
- **Channel 2: On This Day** (Wikipedia's free "On this day" feed). Why: no account or key, fresh content every single day, and it's a nice group thing to read on a big screen ("born today" is a good conversation starter). It shows a source line (CC BY-SA) as Wikipedia's license asks.
- **Controls:** the normal phone remote has no R button, so reset and lap are on + / −, and every screen shows its own hint line.
- **Testing note:** this sandbox can't reach Wikipedia (the network blocks it), so On This Day was tested in the browser with a stand-in feed in Wikipedia's documented format, plus the offline case ("Could not reach Wikipedia"). Please check it once on your laptop with internet.

## Phase 30: Kart pad didn't appear on a real phone
- **What I found:** I could not make it fail here: in a headless browser, one phone and two phones both switch to the kart pad. The old design had one weak spot, though: the console told the phones "show the kart pad" exactly **once**, when the race started. A phone that missed that one message (a Wi-Fi hiccup, the phone briefly pausing the page, a reconnect at the wrong moment) stayed on the normal remote for the whole race. And with rotation lock on, an upright phone only showed "Turn your phone sideways".
- **Fix (so it can't get stuck any more):** the server now remembers which layout the room uses and gives it to every phone that joins or comes back; the console repeats it every 2 s during a race; the pad works with rotation lock on (it turns itself sideways). The `?debug=1` readout on the phone now says where the layout came from and what is really on screen, so if it still happens we'll see why.
- **Protocol:** this adds one field (`layout`) to the join answer and changes no event names. CLAUDE.md says to ask before protocol changes; you asked for a fix without stopping, so I kept it to this backward-compatible addition.
- **The motion-sensor note** ("Motion is allowed, but the sensors send nothing…") no longer appears over the kart pad, because it covered GAS and BRAKE.
- **Steering buttons:** holding LEFT/RIGHT builds up to a full turn in 0.16 s instead of jumping, so a quick tap is a small correction (digital full lock felt twitchy in the tests); letting go straightens in 0.08 s. If both are held, the newest press wins.
- **Browser test:** it needs Playwright, which isn't one of this project's dependencies, so I did not add it to package.json; the test skips itself when Playwright is missing.

## Phase 31: Making Turbo Karts look like a modern kart racer
- **What makes OpenWii's kart game look so good:** mostly its "runtime pack": 102 MB of converted Nintendo models (the drivers and the whole Mario Kart Stadium course), Nintendo item art and fonts. We can't use any of that. Underneath, it also uses good *techniques*, and those I adapted (MIT, credited in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md): PBR Neutral tone mapping (our old ACES curve washed bright colors out, the biggest single difference), multisampled effects, a low close chase camera, a cool fill light with a tight sun shadow, real asphalt (stones, bumps, tyre-worn lanes), streak-shaped drift sparks in three colors, a curling boost flame and sparkle bursts.
- **Feel:** three drift levels (blue 0.8, orange 1.8, purple 3.0 of charge) with mini-turbos of 0.8 / 1.3 / 1.9 s; the turbo now kicks in hard (2.5x acceleration while boosting); steering is smoothed over ~50 ms so nothing jitters; a drift follows your steering more (lean into it, steer out to widen). Landings squash the kart and it springs back; a turbo lifts the nose. CPU karts were checked on all six tracks: they stay on the road as well as before.
- **Characters and karts:** the user asked to replace them with Quaternius characters and Kenney Racing Kit karts. This sandbox cannot reach kenney.nl or quaternius.com (blocked), so the new character pack needs a download by the user (links in the final message). What we already ship is CC0: the karts are Kenney Toy Car Kit vehicles, six characters are Quaternius pirates; the 8 code-built animals are the ones to replace. Kenney's Racing Kit has only race *cars*, no karts, so I kept the Toy Car Kit karts and used the Racing Kit for trackside props (billboards, flags, cones).
- **Comparison screenshots** (OpenWii vs ours, before/after) were made locally with the same window size; the OpenWii one shows Nintendo artwork and is only a reference, it is not in the repository.

## Phase 32: Games from OpenWii
- **Ported:** Fruit Ninja (as **Juicy Slash**) and Alien Attack (as **Alien Strike**): they're good, they're a different kind of game from ours (blade slicing, a rail shooter), and their code is MIT. Renamed so nothing looks like the commercial games; the art is OpenWii's own procedural (code-drawn) art, no Nintendo or other branded assets.
- **Not ported:** Shooting Range. I compared side by side: ours (Target Range) has scoring rings, moving gold targets and penalty targets in a 3D range; OpenWii's is a flat sky with static targets. Ours is better, so porting would be a step back. The same goes for OpenWii's Table Tennis / Drawing, which we already have.
- **Rewiring:** OpenWii games read the phone itself (gyro, motion). Ours get pointer positions and buttons from the console, so: Juicy Slash uses each player's pointer as a blade (up to 4); Alien Strike steers by where you point (or D-pad ◀ ▶ held), because the original needed the phone held flat like a tray, which doesn't fit our standard remote. Phone buzzing on slices/hits goes through our `rumble`.
- **Rules:** Juicy Slash uses real rules (3 missed fruit or a bomb end the run); OpenWii's default was an endless practice mode while they tuned it. Best score is saved in the browser.
- **Testing note:** the browser test uses a slow software renderer, so fruit piled up in the screenshot; on a real GPU it flows normally.

## Phase 33: Phone zoom bug
- **Cause:** the phone page's viewport tag forbids zooming, but iPhone Safari ignores that tag, so a double tap zoomed the page in and it stayed zoomed (the LEFT button then sat off screen).
- **Fix:** `remote/no-zoom.js` stops pinch gestures, swallows a second tap within 0.4 s (except in text boxes), and, if the page is somehow zoomed anyway, snaps it back by re-applying the viewport. The page height also uses the visible height (`100dvh`), so a browser toolbar can't push the bottom buttons off screen.
- **Cursor ("working poorly"):** the phone-side pointer code is unchanged since Phase 21 (the version that felt much better), so I did not change anything for it without a symptom. Asked the user for one (laggy / jumpy / drifting / wrong speed / only in a certain screen) plus the `?debug=1` numbers.

## Phase 34: First real-phone test
- **Cursor going in different directions:** I compared our pipeline with OpenWii's line by line. The pointer code (`gyro-pointer.js`, `orientation.js`) is byte-identical to OpenWii's, and the phone sends the same sensor fields. I could not find a regression, so instead of guessing I added a **Steady cursor** switch: it ignores the learned gyro axis map (the one part that can lock onto a wrong mapping on some phones) and uses the fused orientation only. If Steady fixes it, the cause is the gyro mapping on that phone. If it doesn't, the next test is running OpenWii itself on the same phone (see the final message): if its cursor misbehaves too, it's the phone/browser sensors, not our code.
- **Karts hopping:** a real bug in the rules. After a landing the vertical speed was reset to 0, but on a downhill the road keeps falling away, so the kart took off again the next step and hopped all the way down (desert: 106 hops in 80 s, now ~6, Cloud Gardens 52 → 2). Landing now keeps the road's vertical speed, and a crest only launches you when the road curves away faster than gravity (`CREST_G = 0.9`).
- **Karts "stopping":** every wall touch cost 40% of the speed. A graze along the barrier now barely slows you; only a head-on hit costs speed (up to 40%, scaled by the angle).
- **Alien Strike:** you ran from bullets because nothing said you could shoot. Now a how-to-play screen explains the goal first, and holding A keeps firing.
- **"Copy the original games" (Mario Kart, Fruit Ninja):** I did not recreate Nintendo's or Halfbrick's characters, courses, music or art, even for private use. Their game *mechanics* (item boxes, drifting with mini-turbos, gliders, slicing fruit with combos) are already in our games. For the polished originals' feel, OpenWii (MIT code) can be run next to our console as a separate app.
